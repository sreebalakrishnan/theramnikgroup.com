About the group

Built slowly, on purpose.

The Ramnik Group is privately held and intends to stay that way. Our capital is our own, which means our timeline is our own.

Origins

One business became several.

The group began as a single trading operation run by one family. Growth came the unglamorous way: profits retained, adjacent opportunities taken carefully, and mistakes absorbed rather than passed on.

Over time that discipline produced a portfolio rather than a company. Today the group holds operating businesses across property, hospitality, industry, distribution and investments — but the operating philosophy has not changed since the first one.

We remain deliberately private. We publish no forecasts, chase no league tables, and answer to no external investors.

Milestones

A partial history.

  1. 1978

    The founding trading business is established with a single warehouse and a short customer list.

  2. 1989

    First property acquisition — bought to house the business, kept because the economics were better than the rent.

  3. 1997

    Entry into manufacturing through the acquisition of a specialist engineering firm from its retiring founder.

  4. 2006

    The hospitality division opens its first owned and operated property.

  5. 2014

    Formal group structure established, consolidating the operating companies under a single holding entity.

  6. Today

    Six divisions, third-generation ownership, and the same mandate: buy well, hold long, reinvest.

What we look for

The businesses that fit.

We are specific about what we buy, which lets us move quickly when something genuinely fits.

Criterion

Durable demand

Products or services people will still need in twenty years, sold to customers who are not going away.

Criterion

A real moat

Something that makes the business hard to replace — location, licence, engineering depth, or a reputation earned slowly.

Criterion

Management in place

A team that wants to keep running the business. We are owners and allocators, not a bench of replacement operators.

Criterion

Honest accounts

Cash earnings we can verify, and a balance sheet without surprises hiding in it.

Criterion

Sensible price

We would rather miss a good business than overpay for one. There is always another.

Criterion

A reason to stay

Sellers who care where the business ends up. That alignment matters more to us than speed.

Responsibility

Long horizons make this straightforward.

An owner who never sells inherits every consequence. That simplifies most decisions about how we treat people, places and counterparties.

We employ people we expect to still employ in a decade, so we invest in training and promote from within. We hold buildings we will still own in thirty years, so we maintain them properly and improve their efficiency because the payback period is irrelevant to us.

We pay suppliers on time, deliberately, and expect the same standard from every business in the group.
