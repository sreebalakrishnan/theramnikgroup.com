Size limits — please read

These are bitmaps, not vector artwork. The mark is 613 pixels wide at source, which sets a hard ceiling on how large it can be reproduced:

Print, 300 dpi52 mm74 mm
Read at about a metre, 150 dpi104 mm149 mm

Anything larger — a stage backdrop, a roll-up banner, vehicle livery — needs the original vector file. Ask the group for it rather than scaling these up.

Clear space and minimum size

Keep clear space of at least the height of the hexagon on all four sides. Do not reproduce the mark below 15 mm; under that the ingot and rock detail closes up.

Colour

The logo's three, and the site's palette.

These are two separate sets and they are not interchangeable. The logo carries the colour; the website is deliberately quiet so the photographs can.

In the logo

  • Navy #0C325C The wordmark, the rock in the mark, and the app icon tiles.
  • Amber #E58F03 The sun.
  • Silver #C8C9CD The ingot.

On the website

  • Ink #0a1b2e Body text, dark grounds, the wordmark.
  • Ink 3 #4a6480 Captions and secondary text on light grounds.
  • Paper #FAF9F7 The default ground. Warmer than white on purpose.
  • Paper 3 #E6E3DD Panels and alternating bands.
  • Ember #B4611F Decoration only — rules, fills, underlines. Not for small text.
  • Ember, text #964F18 Small type on light grounds. Darker so it stays readable.
  • Ember, lit #E0A04F The accent on dark grounds only.
  • Green #2F6B45 Sustainability material only. Never as a second brand colour.

One band per page

The mark is three stacked bands and each stands for one part of the group: the sun for solar, the ingot for the ferro alloys, the rock for the mine. The three pages that are about one of those things take their accent colour from their own band, so the page and the logo agree. The strip across the foot of every hero shows all three in the order they appear in the mark, with the page's own band widened and the other two stepped back. The home page leaves all three at full strength, because it is the whole group and not one part of it.

  • Solar — the sun #8B550E / #E9A449 Sustainability. Text value on the left, dark-ground value on the right.
  • Alloys — the ingot #20646F / #8CC0CB Ferro alloys. The band's own silver is far too pale to set type in, so this is the colour the metal is under the shed lights.
  • Mine — the rock #184681 / #6296DA Mining. Pushed brighter than the logo's navy so it reads as an accent and not as body text.

The logo's navy is not the site's ink, and the logo's amber is not the site's ember. They are close enough to look like mistakes for each other and far enough apart to look wrong side by side. The band colours appear undiluted in one place only — the three-part strip under a hero, where they sit on a dark ground and need no adjusting. Everywhere else a page uses the zone values above.

The three ember values are not interchangeable. #B4611F is bright enough to catch the eye as a rule or a fill but too light for small text on paper; #964F18 is the text version; #E0A04F is the only one that works on the dark ground. Swapping them makes text hard to read for anyone with low vision.

These are RGB screen values. For print, ask the printer to convert on their own profile rather than converting them here.

Type

A serif that reads as old, a sans that reads as plain.

Headings are set in a serif because the business is a century old and the type should say so. Everything functional is a plain system sans.

Headings — serif

Four generations of manganese.

Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Book Antiqua, Georgia. Any of these is right. Set headings tight — around 1.1 to 1.2 line height — and do not letter-space them.

Body — serif

The mine at Ramrama produces manganese ore, which is crushed, sized and graded before it is smelted at Sarandi into ferro manganese and silico manganese for the steel industry.

Labels and data — sans

Installed capacity · 34.5 MVA

Small caps with wide tracking, used for eyebrows, captions and figures. This is the only place letter-spacing is used.

Photography

Working photographs, not stock.

Every photograph on the site was taken on site. None of it is bought, and nothing should be.

Do

  • Shoot the work happening — the tap, the haul road, ore stacked by grade.
  • Let it be dusty, wet and lit by whatever light is there.
  • Keep people at a working distance, doing their job.
  • Use the full gallery at theramnikgroup.com/gallery.html as the source.

Don't

  • Use stock photography of other people's plants or mines.
  • Add heavy filters, teal-and-orange grading or lens flare.
  • Make an individual worker the subject of a photograph without their consent.
  • Composite or generate imagery and present it as the operation.

How it should sound

Plain, specific and unhurried. The site says what the operation does and at what scale, and lets that be impressive on its own. No superlatives that cannot be evidenced, no "world-class", no "leading provider of solutions".

Where something is not confirmed, the site says so rather than rounding it up. Please keep that habit — it is the reason the rest of it is credible.

Figures you may quote

Take them from the live site, which is the current version, and nowhere else. The pages carry mine and alloy capacity, installed MVA, solar capacity, product grades, certifications and export destinations.

Do not invent or round figures, and do not take them from a search result — there is material online about other companies with similar names.
