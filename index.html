Who we are

A holding company, not a fund.

We buy good businesses and keep them. There is no exit clock, no forced sale, no fund life to manage around.

What began as a single trading business has grown into a diversified group of operating companies. Each one is run by its own management team, with its own identity and its own customers. What they share is a balance sheet that lets them invest through cycles and an owner that answers to no one but itself.

We are deliberately concentrated. We would rather own a handful of businesses we understand deeply than a portfolio we understand thinly.

How we operate

Four commitments we hold ourselves to.

  1. Own for decades

    We underwrite every acquisition as though we will never sell it, because usually we will not. That single constraint changes what we are willing to buy and what we are willing to pay.

  2. Leave management alone

    The people running each business know it better than we do. We set capital allocation and standards; they run the company. Decision rights sit close to the customer.

  3. Keep the balance sheet boring

    Modest leverage, real liquidity, no covenants that force our hand. Downturns are when patient owners get to buy — but only if they arrive with capacity intact.

  4. Compound quietly

    We reinvest the majority of what our businesses earn. Growth that comes from retained earnings is slower, and it is ours.

For owners and founders

Selling once, to someone who stays.

If you have built something durable and care what happens to it afterwards, we may be the right home for it. We do not break businesses up, we do not strip the team, and we do not resell in five years.

Typical situations we look at: family succession, a founder stepping back, a division that no longer fits its parent, or a partner seeking liquidity without giving up the business.
